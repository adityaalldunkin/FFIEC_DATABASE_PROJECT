Panhandle operator finances 1,200 acres of irrigated farmland

Row-crop and cattle operator · Amarillo · $6.8M land + $400K operating line

A fourth-generation Panhandle operator acquires neighboring irrigated acreage to scale production. They need a farmland term loan plus a seasonal operating line for inputs.

The situation

Agricultural lending is a niche pool — fewer Texas banks report meaningful farmland or ag production balances than CRE or C&I. Amarillo and West Texas have banks with deep ag heritage; portfolio mix shows farm vs production split on RCON1420 and RCON1590.

What the data says

  • Farmland and ag production are among the narrowest lender pools in Texas
  • Specialist threshold (8% portfolio share) identifies true ag banks vs incidental exposure
  • FIRSTBANK SOUTHWEST (Amarillo) is among the largest ICP loan portfolios statewide

Related insights: I-01 I-12

Action plan

  1. Identify banks with farm + ag production >15% combined portfolio share
  2. Prepare 5-year production history, water rights documentation, and crop insurance
  3. Bundle operating line request — ag banks often prefer full-relationship structures
  4. Expect fewer term-sheet options than CRE — relationship depth matters

Questions to ask lenders

  • What advance rate do you use on irrigated vs dryland collateral?
  • Do you offer combined land term and annual operating renewal?
  • How do you stress commodity prices in underwriting?
