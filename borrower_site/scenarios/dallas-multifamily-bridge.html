Dallas sponsor bridges a 48-unit value-add apartment

Experienced multifamily operator · Dallas · $4.2M bridge

A Dallas-based sponsor buys a 48-unit 1980s garden-style community in Oak Cliff with 78% occupancy and a clear rent-gap to renovated comps. They need a 24-month bridge before permanent agency or bank takeout.

The situation

Texas has only about 19 banks with deep multifamily concentration (15%+ of loans). For a $4M+ bridge, you cannot spray-and-pray — you need banks that already hold apartment paper and understand value-add timelines. Dallas has the highest HQ density in the state, but HQ city is only a starting point; branch footprint and portfolio mix matter more.

What the data says

  • Multifamily is a narrow lender pool vs C&I or investor CRE — target specialists
  • Median Texas bank gross loans ~$251M; a $4M bridge is material to ICP community banks
  • 105 Texas banks sit in the $500M–$2B ICP band — ideal for mid-market apartment deals

Related insights: I-01 I-04 I-06 I-07 I-12

Action plan

  1. Build a shortlist of 8–10 Dallas-area and statewide multifamily specialists (8%+ portfolio share)
  2. Lead with rent roll, renovation budget, and stabilized NOI pro forma — not just purchase price
  3. Ask each bank their max hold on multifamily bridge and whether they offer mini-perm takeout
  4. Compare 3 term sheets; multifamily specialists often price better than generalist banks

Questions to ask lenders

  • What is your maximum multifamily bridge exposure per sponsor?
  • Do you require pre-leasing or renovation completion milestones?
  • Can you provide takeout on the same relationship after stabilization?
