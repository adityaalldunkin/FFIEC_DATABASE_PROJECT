1. Executive snapshot
Lenni is a Texas technology company, founded in 2026 by Doak C. Dunkin, the third generation of a family business that has spent roughly fifty years inside community banking. It sits under the ALL Dunkin family-enterprise umbrella and is explicitly not a product of Dunkin & Associates (the family’s flagship insurance-to-banks business) — it is its own standalone company.
At its foundation, Lenni’s product is the digital handshake for community banks: a white-labeled borrower-and-lender hub that slots in between a community bank’s website and its existing Loan Origination System (LOS), putting a real lender at the front of every online loan inquiry. The bank keeps every page, workflow, Core, and LOS it already has. Lenni occupies the gap where a missing branch visit used to live.

In the foundational documents, Lenni is a single product aimed at one market: Texas community banks. 5. Why Lenni exists — the gap
When a customer walks into a branch, a lender greets them, answers questions, and walks them through the application. That human moment is the community bank’s competitive edge. The instant the customer chooses to apply online instead, the lender disappears and the customer is alone with a form. Lenni puts the lender back at the front of the online experience — same conversation, same guidance, no change to the bank’s LOS or Core.
The numbers that make the gap defensible
	•	267 of ~360 Texas community banks (~74%) have no online consumer loan application at all — not even a web form.
	•	45 present only a PDF at the “Apply” link.
	•	The remainder use an LOS-vendor online application (MeridianLink, Encino, FIS, Jack Henry SilverLake).
	•	Zero have direct messaging with a real lender. Commercial applications online are effectively zero.
	•	2 commercial real-estate (CRE) leads in one week came to a real community banker from nothing but a “Contact Us” form — demand exists; banks aren’t capturing it.

The volume bet, in dollars
	•	5+ incremental loans per month per bank — Lenni’s conservative floor, defensible at loan committee.
	•	$500K+ average loan size when CRE is in the mix.
	•	Five incremental CRE loans/month → $1M+ in year-one net interest income, before origination fees, deposits, or cross-sell.
	•	Deployed in 2–3 weeks, with no integration beyond a button on a webpage.
	•	First Financial Bank anecdote: one top loan officer is expected to fund $20M/month.

8. Brand voice — the operational rules
Voice is unusually load-bearing at Lenni: the brand is sold banker-to-banker, and a single off-key word can break the peer posture. There are two coexisting voice systems — the community-bank / Convey voice and the Lenni umbrella voice. They share DNA (concrete nouns, declarative, the customer is the hero) but differ in signature pattern and taglines.
8.1 The signature line patterns
Community-bank / Convey pattern:
[concrete community-banking noun] + [active verb] + online
	•	Open your digital branch.
	•	The handshake goes online.
	•	Real lenders. Real conversations. Online.
	•	Online lending with a lender in it.
Lenni umbrella pattern:
[Active verb] + your clients + where they are.
	•	Serve your clients where they are.
	•	Meet them there.
	•	Stay where your clients are.
8.2 The voice rules
Community-bank / Convey — six rules:
	•	The banker is the subject of every sentence. The bank is the hero; Lenni is the tool.
	•	Concrete nouns only. Lender, branch, desk, handshake, conversation, community, neighbor, paper trail. Never abstractions like experience, journey, transformation, solution, platform.
	•	Declarative, not defensive. We open things; we don’t prevent loss. Avoid “doesn’t have to,” “won’t lose,” “stay” framings.
	•	“Online” is the modifier, never the headline. It is the where, not the what.
	•	The Desk and The Handshake are bank-facing only. Never use these terms with borrowers; borrower-facing copy is plain (“talk to a real lender”).
	•	Time trust: don’t promise instant; promise honest. Visible business hours, not fake 24/7.
Lenni umbrella — seven rules:
	•	The firm is the actor; the client is where the firm goes. Lenni is never the protagonist.
	•	Concrete professional-service nouns beat abstract tech nouns. Client, firm, professional, banker, attorney, CPA, conversation, handshake, file, engagement beat platform, system, solution, journey.
	•	Declarative beats defensive. Say where the firm goes with Lenni; never what the firm “won’t lose.”
	•	Speak about relevance, not heritage. Family lineage belongs on the About page and in contract preambles — never in hero copy or campaigns.
	•	Meet the client; don’t replace the firm. Lenni is the bridge, never the destination.
	•	AI augments the professional; it does not replace them. The firm’s professionals stay the irreplaceable element.
	•	No PE vocabulary, ever. Inherited from ALL Dunkin. Never “portfolio company,” “dry powder,” “LP/GP.”

11. Pricing and the economic story
Pricing
	•	Original float: ~$500/month for 5 users, plus $100/user beyond.
	•	Revised target (post-FrogSlayer kickoff): $2,500–$5,000/month base, with bolt-ons over time. FrogSlayer pushed back on user-count caps, so V1 has no user-count caps (don’t create a barrier to usage).

Retention of high-value relationships in the AI age. Every Lenni product protects the firm’s earned book of business as those clients move into AI-augmented work. Lenni’s cost is small; the cost of losing one high-value client to a more AI-fluent competitor is not.

12. Compliance and trust posture
Lenni’s compliance pitch is built for community-bank scrutiny. Doak’s philosophy: “If we show up with a phone book, we won’t have problems. If we show up with a one-sheet ‘we’re compliant,’ we’re fucked.” Build the thick package; lean on FrogSlayer’s SOC 2 posture for V1; pursue Lenni’s own attestation post-launch.
The five-point posture
	•	Lenni doesn’t underwrite — every credit decision stays with the bank’s lenders.
	•	Encryption everywhere — TLS 1.2+ in transit, AES-256 at rest, managed keys.
	•	SOC 2 in progress — Type I in ~6 months, Type II in ~18, leveraging FrogSlayer’s existing controls.
	•	Audit trail by default — every consent, message, document, and system event is timestamped and immutable.
	•	Bank owns the data — the bank is the data controller; Lenni is the service provider. MSA + DPA spell it out.

Compliance tags and open items
Tags: GLBA service provider · FTC Safeguards Rule · 10DLC registered · TCPA-compliant SMS · E-SIGN compliant
Open items: KYC requirements (research-only in V1); MSA / Privacy-attorney validation; full audit-export tooling for exam packages (manual in V1).
Certification nuance: FrogSlayer holds SOC 2 Type II, but Lenni-the-product cannot inherit it directly; Lenni will need its own. In the interim, the bank-compliance pitch leans on FrogSlayer’s posture.
At the umbrella level the same posture generalizes: “privaCy by design — Lenni doesn’t read the firm’s client data (no mining, no training, no analysis)”; the firm is the data controller; the MSA and DPA name Lenni the service provider. Several of these claims (the “doesn’t read the data” claim, exact encryption protocols, audit-trail mechanics, and umbrella-level MSA/DPA language) are flagged as needing engineering / legal sign-off before external publishing.

13. The sales philosophy — one bank per market
Lenni operates on a one-bank-per-market model. Each market zone (roughly a 25-mile radius around a population center) gets one Lenni partner. If the #1 bank in that zone passes, Lenni moves to #2, and so on. This is not a manufactured pressure tactic — it is how the product works. Each deployment is white-labeled; signing two competing banks in the same market would undermine both. The exclusivity is real.
We are not selling. We are selecting.
— The core sales mindset
Every outreach, call, and demo communicates: we chose you, we did our research, we think you’re the right bank for this market — but we won’t wait forever; if you’re not interested, your competitor across town will be. Respectful, never aggressive, time-bound, and real. The selling season runs roughly August 1 through the end of October; banks go quiet from Christmas through New Year.
